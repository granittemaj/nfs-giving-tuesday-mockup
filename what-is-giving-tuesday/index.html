What is Giving Tuesday and how can you take part

The short answer

Giving Tuesday is a global movement that encourages people to contribute to the lives of others. Its annual giving day falls on December 1 in 2026. You can donate to a charity, volunteer, offer a useful skill or help someone in your community. The movement does not require a particular donation amount or a public declaration of generosity. 01, 02

For someone encountering Giving Tuesday for the first time, the number of appeals can make a straightforward idea feel complicated. There are charities asking for support, employers announcing activities and friends sharing causes. The useful starting point is to decide what you want to contribute and who would benefit, then choose a way to follow through.

When is Giving Tuesday in 2026

Giving Tuesday is Tuesday, December 1, 2026. It takes place on the Tuesday after U.S. Thanksgiving, following the Black Friday and Cyber Monday shopping period. That relationship to the calendar explains why it sometimes falls in November and sometimes in December. The official GivingTuesday participation page confirms the 2026 date. 01, 03

A charity may open its appeal earlier, and a school or workplace may choose another day for an activity. Those arrangements belong to the individual organizer. Check the details of an event or matching offer rather than assuming every campaign starts and ends at the same time. If you are coordinating people in different countries, state the relevant time zone alongside any actual deadline.

How Giving Tuesday began

GivingTuesday began in 2012 at New York’s 92nd Street Y and its Belfer Center for Innovation and Social Impact. It subsequently became an independent nonprofit supporting a wider global movement. Its own history describes an idea that grew from a day encouraging generosity into activity throughout the year. 02

The distinction between the organization and the movement matters when you give. GivingTuesday helps support the movement; participating charities run their own appeals and receive donations through their own arrangements. Not For Sale’s hub is one charity’s contribution to the day, not the official headquarters of GivingTuesday.

What counts as taking part

Participation can be financial, practical or relational. A gift to an organization helps fund its work. A volunteer contribution supplies agreed time or expertise. Helping a neighbor may meet a need that never appears in a formal campaign. These actions serve different purposes, and you do not have to convert every kind act into a fundraising total.

For a financial gift, start with the Giving Tuesday donation guide. For time or skills, use the volunteering guide. Neither route is a test of how much you care. Choose a contribution that fits your circumstances and meets an actual need.

A first plan that does not become a project

Begin with a person, organization or issue you want to support. If the need is specific, ask the recipient what would help. If you are choosing a charity, read enough to explain its work in your own words. You should be able to describe more than the problem: you should understand what the organization does in response.

Next, decide what you can offer. A realistic commitment includes the time needed to arrange it. Offering two hours of professional work, for example, may require a short briefing and a handover as well as the task itself. If your available time is uncertain, say so before someone relies on your offer.

Finally, choose the next action and a date. That might be making a donation after reviewing the charity, contacting a volunteer coordinator or discussing an activity with colleagues. Keep the plan small enough that you can complete it without needing a new account, a complicated event or a public campaign.

If you want to involve other people

Give people a clear invitation rather than a general instruction to “do something.” Explain the cause, the proposed action and whether money is involved. Allow a genuine choice. A colleague should not have to disclose their finances to decline a donation, and a child should not feel that the size of a family contribution measures kindness.

A simple invitation could say: “I’m using Giving Tuesday to learn more about this cause and support the work if I can. Here is the guide I found useful. If you would like to join me, we could choose one action together.” That leaves room for a conversation and avoids promising a result you cannot substantiate.

Taking part beyond the United States

The movement has national and local organizers in many countries. The official GivingTuesday global network is a starting point for finding the movement where you live. Local activity may use different languages, names and arrangements. 04

For donations across borders, check the currency and the organization receiving the money. Tax treatment does not automatically travel with a charity’s registration in another country. This is particularly relevant when someone encounters a U.S. appeal from elsewhere in the world. The day is international; the rules attached to a particular gift remain specific.

Why Not For Sale takes part

Giving Tuesday creates an opportunity to examine issues that often reach people through fragments: a headline about exploitation, an image of forest loss, an appeal for emergency help. Our contribution is to explain the relationship between human freedom and the conditions in which people live and work, then offer a clear route to support our mission.

Start with Giving Tuesday against human trafficking or Giving Tuesday against ecocide. Both lead to the same broader question: how can people build lives with genuine choices, supported by livelihoods and environments that can sustain them?

Giving Tuesday questions
Do I have to register?+

There is no central registration required simply to take part in the movement. A particular event, volunteer role or fundraising service may have its own registration requirements. Check those with the organizer. 03

Is Giving Tuesday only for charities?+

No. Individuals, informal groups, schools and businesses can participate. A personal act of help can be part of the day even when no charity receives money.

Do I have to give money?+

No. Time, useful skills, attention and practical help are also ways to contribute. If you offer something to an organization, ask what it needs before arranging the activity.

Can I donate before December 1?+

Yes. Giving is not confined to the annual date. If an appeal includes a limited matching offer, check whether an earlier gift qualifies under that offer’s terms.

Who receives my donation?+

That depends on the route you use. Read the recipient and payment details. A gift through Not For Sale’s official giving page is separate from donating to the GivingTuesday organization itself.

Do all donations get doubled?+

No. Matching requires a specific funder or employer program, with its own conditions. The Giving Tuesday name does not establish a match.

What should I do after the day?+

Keep any donation confirmation, complete the work you agreed to and thank the people involved. If you promised an update, provide it. Consider continuing only when the commitment fits your circumstances and the recipient’s needs.

Where should I start if I am still unsure?+

Read the ideas guide and choose one action you could reasonably complete. You can learn about a cause before deciding whether to give.
